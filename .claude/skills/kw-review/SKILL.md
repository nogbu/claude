## Keyword list reviews
- Show keyword lists in alphabetical order unless I ask otherwise, 5 items per batch, labeled "items X–Y of N" (this wins over walkthrough's one item per message); ask about the batch's items one card at a time.  
- Table columns: "Negative keyword", Match, Company (company lists only), "Proposed negative keyword" when there's a replacement, and Rec. Every question comes with its table.  
- One keyword per row, each with its own recommendation. Never group word forms under one item number.  
- Say "negate" a term, never "keep" it. "all" = accept every recommendation in the batch.  
- If a reply is ambiguous, or differs from the recommendation in a surprising way, confirm it before recording.  
- Every removed or un-negated keyword goes on the revisit-later list.  
- Research every term with web sources before presenting it. Mark it unconfirmed only if research doesn't settle it.  
- Walk through any research findings that change a list before resuming regular batches.
- Multiple choice: A = Negate and S = Don't negate (revisit later), with B, C… for any other choice, instead of strictly sequential letters. The letters go in the decision card's option labels (e.g. "A: Negate").  
- Save review decisions to the list's content file every 15 items and when a finding or list finishes.

## Negative keywords
- For a single word, use BROAD (BROAD and PHRASE behave identically there).  
- Propose singular/plural and one-word/two-word variants only where commonly searched. Exception: when a negative belongs to a fixed set (one per state, member company or plan), propose the whole set, including members not yet seen in search terms. Re-check them against search terms data later.  
- Domain negatives use ".com" (e.g. "dentalplans.com"), not " com".  
- Test every list against the positive keywords of every active campaign it may be attached to. Fix any conflict on the negative side.  
- Avoid a negative word only if it would block searches from people the ads are meant to reach. Being an everyday English word is not by itself a reason to avoid it.  
- A negative that blocks some relevant searches can stay when the irrelevant searches it blocks far outnumber them. Only irrelevant searches that could actually trigger our ads count toward that volume.  
- Judge a negative by whether its word signals the searcher isn't shopping for what the client sells. Before calling a word redundant, check how the other lists on the same campaigns cover the searches it blocks: products covered only as PHRASE or EXACT leak when a word such as "org" or "website" is inserted.
