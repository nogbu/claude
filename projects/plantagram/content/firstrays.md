## First Rays learnings: Semi-Hydroponics and Plant Nutrition sections (read 2026-09-25; LECA-only material removed 2026-09-26)

First Rays writes about orchids. Applying it to anthuriums is my inference; both are mostly epiphytic, with roots adapted to airy, wet-dry conditions.

**Semi-hydro setup and watering**

- Pots: closed-bottom container with two ¼-inch holes on the sides, about 1 inch up from the bottom.  
- **Flood the pot rapidly to the top at every watering and let it drain to the hole level.** Only topping up the reservoir concentrates minerals and waste and eventually harms roots. Letting the pot dry out defeats the method.  
- Water before the pot dries completely; established plants can't be overwatered in S/H.  
- A dry-looking top layer ("dry line") is normal. Wicking weakens with height, and warmth, airflow, light, and low humidity push the line lower. Squat pots stay wetter.  
- Wait at least 30 minutes (ideally an hour) between back-to-back waterings so roots can take up the first dose.

**Moving plants into S/H**

- Repot when new roots are starting. Old roots adapted to the previous medium will die; the plant has to grow new ones.  
- Move straight to final conditions; don't use an in-between step, which forces two adaptation cycles.  
- Clean off old organic media and trim dead roots. Fill the pot about ⅓ with substrate, spread the roots, and add substrate in small amounts while shaking the pot.  
- Aftercare: shade, warmth, high humidity (loose clear bag), bottom heat, consistently moist medium. Keep stress low for 4–6 weeks. With bottom heat, rootless plants can establish in about 2 weeks.  
- Don't use S/H as a rescue for a plant you can't already grow; it only changes moisture and media, not light, humidity, temperature, or feeding.

**Temperature**

- Evaporation from wet LECA cools the root zone: 3.4°F below a 74°F room at 35% RH, more in cooler rooms. Warm growers suffer most. *(inference)* This likely applies to anthuriums, which prefer warm roots, and to other wet inert media.  
- Fixes: raise humidity; a heat mat (about +15°F root zone, 17 W for a 9×19" mat); an evaporation barrier (clear plastic disc about 1" smaller than the pot, slit to the center, fitted around the plant base; water right through it).  
- For cool growers, clay pots add evaporative cooling. Heat without enough light gives soft, leggy growth.

**Fertilizer concentration and frequency**

- Target about 75–125 ppm nitrogen per week in total, split however you like: 25 ppm at 3–4 waterings a week, 100 ppm weekly, or 200 ppm every two weeks. Don't feed less often than every two weeks.  
- For S/H and daily watering, First Rays recommends **about 25 ppm N at every watering** (the basics article says 30–50 ppm). The author's own routine is about 35 ppm N three times a week.  
- "Weakly, weekly": frequent dilute feeding beats occasional strong doses, and more fertilizer never helps. Excess nitrogen suppresses blooming.  
- Quick dosing math for 100 ppm N: 8 ÷ %N = teaspoons per gallon, or 9.2 ÷ %N = mL per liter. This is written for dry powders.  
- Stick with one formula for at least 6 months before judging it.  
- There's no such thing as a "bloom booster." Extra phosphorus doesn't add flowers; lower nitrogen and good light, temperature, and humidity do.

**Potassium, calcium, and magnesium**

- First Rays' main thesis: standard fertilizers supply far more potassium than epiphytes get in nature (under 10 ppm in rainwater vs. 70–250 ppm in typical feeds), and excess K competes with Ca and Mg. Its K-Lite fertilizer (12-1-1 + 10 Ca + 3 Mg) was built on this.  
- Targets relative to nitrogen: calcium 30–75% of N; magnesium 20–50% of N.  
- Pure water (RO, distilled, rain) **must** have Ca and Mg supplemented. Tap or well water may not need it; get a water analysis first.  
- Epsom salt is about 10% Mg: ⅛ tsp/gal at every feeding, ¾–1 tsp/gal monthly, or about 1 Tbsp/gal quarterly in plain water.

**pH**

- First Rays argues that **adjusting the pH of the feed solution is mostly unnecessary** unless you're badly overdosing. LECA has almost no cation exchange capacity, so nutrients stay available. Roots adjust pH around themselves.  
- Nitrate-heavy feeds push the root zone more alkaline and ammonium feeds more acidic. The author fixed yellowing by switching from 2% to 15% ammoniacal nitrogen.  
- To check what roots actually see: drench the pot, wait 60 minutes, pour distilled water through, and test about 50 mL of runoff.

**Additives**

- Rooting hormones (NAA, IBA) help mainly at repotting. The author's trial showed a 17–24% growth gain, but little benefit when overall culture is already good. Overuse can deform flowers and, in extreme cases, kill plants; effects fade over 2–3 weeks after stopping.  
- Hormone dose: about 2 drops per gallon; 1 tsp/gal is overdosing. Synthetic hormones break down with heat and light; they keep about a year refrigerated in the dark, only months at room temperature.  
- **SUPERthrive:** First Rays says it contains NAA (a synthetic hormone). The current label you sent lists only vitamin B1 and kelp extract. The article may predate a reformulation. My bottle is the new formula (confirmed 2026-10-01), so this hormone guidance doesn't apply to it.  
- Seaweed extracts vary a lot by species and processing. First Rays favors *Ecklonia maxima* (Kelpak): 1 Tbsp/gal (1:250) every 3–4 weeks, or every 2 weeks when pushing growth; mix it with fertilizer; never dilute past 1:500. For rootless plants, soak 1–2 hours, then use it for the next two waterings. First Rays sells Kelpak.  
- Mega-dose boron/molybdenum products (Mega Thrive) are risky: boron is phytotoxic when overdone, and high Mo makes plants toxic to pets.  
- Beneficial microbes (*Bacillus*, *Trichoderma*) suppress root pathogens and help roots. First Rays advises avoiding excessive sterilization. Double probiotic doses if the water is chlorinated.

**Pests & Ailments and Rooting Hormones articles (read 2026-10-01)**

- Root rot (Pests & Ailments, read 2026-10-01): it comes from root suffocation, not water itself. Recovery keys: high humidity, warmth, subdued light.  
- Don't fertilize until the plant is established in its new pot; keep fertilizer out of rescue soaks because it draws water from the plant (read 2026-10-01).  
- Rootless rescue (read 2026-10-01): soak in Kelpak 1–2 Tbsp/gal for hours, presoak the medium, water in with it, use 1 Tbsp/gal for the next two waterings, tent with a clear bag, and add bottom heat.  
- Stimulants (Rooting Hormones, read 2026-10-01): wait about a month between doses; hormones help mostly at repotting; Kelpak is more stable than synthetic hormones.

**Measurement and storage**

- Don't trust TDS/ppm meter readings across fertilizers; the EC-to-ppm conversion varies by formula and meter. Build a calibration for each fertilizer you use, or work in ppm N and EC.  
- Epsom salt adds about 7 µS/cm per ppm Mg.  
- Dry fertilizer powders keep for years sealed. Liquid concentrates keep about 3 months sealed and dark. Diluted organic additives (Kelpak, probiotics) should be used the same day.

**Applying this to my products** *(inference; my math)*

- **TPS One nitrogen:** the label gives 3.2% N and 2.5 lb per quart (about 1.19 g/mL). That works out to roughly **10 ppm N per mL/gal**. So First Rays' 25 ppm S/H rate ≈ 2.5 mL/gal of TPS One every watering, and 50 ppm ≈ 5 mL/gal. TPS's label minimum (5 mL/gal) matches First Rays' upper S/H range.  
- **Potassium:** TPS One supplies slightly more potassium than nitrogen (K₂O 4.1% vs. N 3.2%), which is the kind of high-K ratio First Rays warns about. Adding Signal (all K) or Silica Gold (K silicate) raises K further.  
- **Ca/Mg:** by First Rays' targets, TPS One's Ca is at the low end (about 31% of N) and its Mg is below the range (about 16% of N). TPS says extra CalMag isn't needed; First Rays' ratios suggest a little Mg may help, especially with RO or distilled water. The two sources disagree here.  
- **pH:** TPS says to adjust to 5.8–6.2; First Rays says solution pH adjustment is mostly unnecessary in LECA. The two sources disagree here too.  
- **Sterilizers:** First Rays' advice to avoid excessive sterilization reinforces the earlier note about H₂O₂ and Physan 20 conflicting with TPS One, Root Boost, and HygroZyme.
