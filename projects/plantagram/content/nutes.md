# nutes

Added 2026-09-26. **Exclusively for juvenile and mature anthuriums in DIY pon.** Flowering anthuriums get flutes instead (products/flutes). Per gallon of base water. Measured EC values are mine; the rest are Claude's estimates from my per-mL measurements (CalMag \~160 µS/cm per mL, TPS One \~115 (inference from the measured total), Root Boost \~25; HygroZyme +5 µS/cm at 8 mL, measured 925 → \~930).

## Recipe (per gallon)

Add one product at a time and stir between additions. Never combine concentrates.

| Order | Product | Rate per gallon | EC after adding | Basis |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Base water | 1 gal | \~0 µS/cm *(measured)* | Adds nothing to EC |
| 2 | API Water Conditioner | 4 drops | \~0 µS/cm *(measured)* | Neutralizes chloramine to protect the microbes |
| 3 | TPS CalMag Complete | 2 mL | \~325 µS/cm *(measured)* | Required with base water. Adds nitrogen without phosphorus. |
| 4 | TPS One | 5 mL | \~900 µS/cm *(est.)* | TPS label minimum |
| 5 | TPS Root Boost | 1 mL | \~925 µS/cm *(measured)* | TPS feedchart rate |
| 6 | HygroZyme | 8 mL | \~930 µS/cm *(measured)* | Label rate (8–10 mL/gal) |
| 7 | TPS pH Up | 6 drops | — | Target pH 5.5–6.2 |

TPS One's step is likely \~115 µS/cm per mL rather than \~96, since the measured total runs \~95 over the estimates *(inference)*.  
*(inference; Claude's label math)* Estimated composition: about 84 ppm N, 49 P, 55 K, 49 Ca, 20 Mg. For comparison, the Dutch standard feed for anthurium (de Kreij/Straver, via Haifa) is 800 µS/cm, \~74 ppm N, \~22 P, 117 K, 40 Ca, 17 Mg, with a root-environment EC of 1,000 µS/cm.

## pH

- Add 6 drops of TPS pH Up (step 7), stir, and check pH. Target 5.5–6.2 (TPS feedchart and Foliage Factory).  
- If pH is still outside 5.5–6.2, adjust in tiny steps: base water has almost no buffering, so each drop moves pH a lot. Stir, wait a minute, re-check.

## Weekly routine

1. **Mix a fresh batch.** Log EC after each step until a few batches are recorded, then just check the final EC.  
2. **Flood every pot.** Over the sink, tip the pot to drain the old reservoir out the slots, then pour fresh solution through the top until it runs out the slots. Empty the outer pot and set the pot back in it.  
3. **Day 3–4:** if a reservoir has dropped noticeably, top it up with **plain base water** (Foliage Factory). Usually applies to pots on the fan shelves.  
4. **Day 7, before flushing:** measure the reservoir runoff EC.

## Adjusting from the day-7 reading

| Day-7 runoff | What to change |
| :---- | :---- |
| \~1,000–1,200 µS/cm | Nothing. On target (Dutch root zone \~1,000). |
| Over 1,200, or brown tips appear | Add the mid-week plain-water top-up, or drop TPS One to 4.5 mL |
| Flat or below fresh EC, and older leaves pale or new leaves come in smaller | Raise TPS One to 5.5–6 mL |

Change one thing at a time, and judge the result by new growth.

## Monthly flush

Once a month, on the day-7 watering, in place of that week's nutes. Pon only. Then refill with putes' day-7 batch (no RapidStart).

### Soak water (per gallon)

Conditioned tap water. Add one product at a time and stir.

| Order | Product | Rate per gallon | EC after adding | Basis |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Tap water | 1 gal | Not measured yet | Warm to about 78–80°F (inference) |
| 2 | API Tap Water Conditioner | 4 drops | Not measured yet | Dosing rate (config.plantagram) |
| 3 | SLF-100 | 10 mL | Not measured yet | Maker's flush rate, 2× the media rate |

### Rinse water (per gallon)

Conditioned tap water. Measure its EC once; it's the stop rule's target. Neither water gets a pH adjustment *(inference; First Rays)*.

| Order | Product | Rate per gallon | EC after adding | Basis |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Tap water | 1 gal | Not measured yet | Warm to about 78–80°F (inference) |
| 2 | API Tap Water Conditioner | 4 drops | Not measured yet | Dosing rate (config.plantagram) |

**For ½ gallon:** soak water 2 drops conditioner and 5 mL SLF-100; rinse water 2 drops conditioner.

### Use

1. **Before:** measure the day-7 runoff EC as usual.  
2. **Drain the reservoir:** over the sink, tip the pot until the slots are the lowest point, with a hand over the pon. Empty and wipe the decorative outer pot.  
3. **Flood and soak:** set the pot in the soak container (one container, fresh soak water for each plant) and pour soak water through the top until the level reaches the pon surface, below the crown. If pumice starts to float, stop just below it *(inference)*.  
4. **Soak in the cabinet:** measure the soak water's EC at the start and every 30 minutes. End when two measurements in a row are within about 50 µS/cm *(inference)*; cap at 2 hours (First Rays' longest full soak).  
5. **Drain:** lift the pot out, tip it over the sink, and dump the soak water.  
   **Before the next plant:** rinse the container with tap water, then wipe every inside surface wet with Physan 20 at 14.8 mL per gallon (7.4 mL per ½ gallon) of tap water and leave it wet 10 minutes. Rinse it with tap water so no Physan carries into the next soak, then mix fresh soak water *(adapted from Physan's used-pot directions: 10-minute soak at 1 Tbsp/gal, no rinse; inference)*.  
6. **Rinse in cycles:** over the sink, flood from the top with rinse water until it runs out the slots, set the pot back in its outer pot for 30 minutes (First Rays' minimum between waterings), then tip it over the sink and catch about 50 mL of the water that drains out *(adapted from First Rays' runoff test, which waits 60 minutes and pours distilled water through; inference)*. Log EC and pH. Repeat until the drained water is within about 100 µS/cm of the rinse water *(inference)*.  
7. **If EC won't come down** after three rinse cycles, repeat the whole flush the next day: the maker's flush runs daily for up to three days.  
8. **Check roots** through the clear wall: firm white or green is good; brown or mushy means rot *(inference)*.  
9. **Refill:** over the sink, flood with putes' day-7 batch until it runs out the slots, then return the pot to its outer pot.

### Decisions and why

- **One day, in cycles** (agreed 2026-10-02): leaching studies favor wet–wait–drain cycles over one continuous pour, and the soak is the longest contact period. The studies were on soils, not pumice.  
- **One soak container, cleaned between plants** (agreed 2026-10-06): the outer pots aren't watertight, and fresh soak water plus a cleaned container keeps one plant's root rot from reaching the next *(inference)*.  
- **Conditioned tap water for soak and rinse** (agreed 2026-10-02): saves ZeroWater; tap minerals likely reduce what the soak pulls out slightly *(inference)*.  
- **SLF-100 at 10 mL/gal, soak only** (agreed 2026-10-02): the maker's flush rate (retail label copies say up to 3×). No source describes an SLF-100 soak, and no independent data shows SLF-100 removes mineral salts; the water does that.  
- **No H₂O₂ pass** (agreed 2026-10-02): it would likely kill the *Bacillus* that TPS One and Root Boost leave in the pon *(inference)*.  
- **Stop by EC, not pass count:** pots range from 3 to 6.5 in (setup), so each holds very different amounts of pon and water.  
- **The zeolite isn't emptied:** water alone pulls little off it, and its stored nutrients are a buffer, not the buildup *(inference from zeolite and pumice studies)*.  
- **Refill is putes' day-7 batch** (agreed 2026-10-08): no separate refill recipe. Its Kelpak is the monthly stimulant; RapidStart stays out, since established plants don't need it.  
- **Left out of the refill** (agreed 2026-10-08): RootShield PLUS+ WP (BioWorks labels it every 8–10 weeks in hydro, and its chart lists *B. subtilis* and *B. amyloliquefaciens* products, species in TPS One and Root Boost, as tank-mix incompatible); Silica Gold (labeled weekly, TPS wants silica solutions changed every 4–5 days, raises pH); SNS 209 (no monthly preventive schedule from SNS; use it on its label schedule when pests appear).  
- **Replaces** the earlier monthly flush (base water plus SLF-100 at ½ tsp/gal, then nutes the following week) (2026-10-02).

### Sources behind the flush

- [SLF-100 maker page](https://socascade.com/product.php): rates and the daily flush for up to three days; no soak or contact time.  
- [Monster Gardens SLF-100 label copy](https://monstergardens.com/products/south-cascade-organics-slf-100): flush up to 3×, "thoroughly soaking the system" daily for three days.  
- [Intermittent vs. continuous leaching (Sustainability, 2024)](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/23/10256) and the [University of Minnesota salt removal review](https://conservancy.umn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/80248bbd-4539-4ce0-9801-61ed6a38c7da/content): cycles remove more salt per unit of water.  
- [Kerr 1983, Leaching of salt from container media](https://mountainscholar.org/items/bafecec7-2607-4102-968a-2da5deeaaa41/full): most old solution leaves within 1–1.5 pot volumes.  
- [Jaskūnas 2015, potassium on clinoptilolite](https://lmaleidykla.lt/ojs/index.php/chemija/article/view/5865) and [pumice and ammonium (ISHS, 2019)](https://ishs.org/ishs-article/1266_34/): why water doesn't empty the zeolite.  
- [First Rays, Semi-Hydroponics](https://firstrays.com/free-cultural-information/semi-hydroponics/) and [Rescue Plants with No Roots](https://firstrays.com/free-cultural-information/pests-ailments/diseases-ailments/rescue-plants-with-no-roots/): flooding, waits between waterings, the runoff test, 1–2 hour soaks.

## Optional

- **Root Boost** matters most after repotting or while a plant is growing new roots. For settled plants it can be left out, which trims a little phosphorus.  
- **Evaporation barriers** (First Rays) on pots on the fan shelves slow water loss.

## Keep out of the reservoir

- **TPS Silica Gold:** removed at my request.  
- **TPS Signal:** removed at my request.  
- **H₂O₂ and Physan 20:** they kill the microbes in TPS One and Root Boost.  
- **RapidStart:** not needed for established plants; GH notes that growers taper off once a strong root mass forms. It does a different job from Root Boost (see Root Boost vs. RapidStart in inventory).  
- **Dip 'N Grow:** for dipping cuttings only.

## Notes

- **Zeolite in pon** can take up nutrients and release them later (Foliage Factory). Go by the runoff readings, not the numbers on paper.  
- **Light:** measured 65–85 PPFD at leaf height (about 3.3–4.3 DLI over 14 hours, Claude's calculation), which supports this moderate feed. If lights are added or plants move somewhere brighter, revisit the TPS One dose.  
- **Known limitation:** TPS One's formula keeps P high and K below N. This recipe keeps both in check, but only a different base fertilizer would fix the ratio. Kelpak's K eases it in the flush refill only.  
- **Kelpak monthly, in the flush refill only** (agreed 2026-10-06; via putes' day-7 batch since 2026-10-08): First Rays' default for established plants (every 2 weeks only when pushing growth); LECA Addict also uses it monthly. At 65–85 PPFD, light limits growth, so pushing likely gains little *(inference)*. Mix fresh (diluted Kelpak breaks down within days); refrigerate the opened bottle.

## Open questions

- **HygroZyme EC:** my measurements disagree: +5 at 8 mL/gal (this recipe, 925 → \~930) vs +30 at 10 mL/gal (putes batch, 2026-10-02). Measure again.  
- **CalMag EC:** 2 mL/gal measured 325 here and 375 on 2026-10-02 (putes ½-gal batch, base water 0). Likely a short half gallon or meter variation; measure the next batch to settle it.  

## Sources behind this recipe

- TPS product labels and feedcharts (favored); CalMag Complete dosed the same as CalMag OAC, since TPS publishes the same feedchart for both.  
- Dutch standard for *Anthurium andreanum* in rockwool/peat, as published on the [Haifa Group page](https://www.haifa-group.com/fertilization-anthurium-andereanum-sandy-soil-or-soilless) (N. Straver 1994; C. de Kreij et al. 1992). Prepared for Dutch low-transpiration greenhouse conditions.  
- [Univ. of Hawaii Horticulture Digest #102](https://www3.ctahr.hawaii.edu/tpss/digest/hd102/hd102_5.html) (Lichty, Kratky, Higaki, Nakamura, Moniz): 'Kalapana' anthurium in a non-circulating hydroponic system, cinder medium, 80% shade; started at 1,000 µS/cm and 80 ppm N, EC fell to \~500 by month 6.  
- [Özçelik & Özkan 2002, Acta Hort. 573](https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2002.573.11): anthurium in pumice; "susceptible to salinity," requires EC 1.0–1.5 mS/cm.  
- [Foliage Factory semi-hydro guide](https://foliage-factory.com/blogs/plant-care/fertilizing-houseplants-semi-hydro-guide) (retailer, no author/date): EC bands tied to light; velvet anthuriums react more to stale solution and pH drift than to slightly low EC.
