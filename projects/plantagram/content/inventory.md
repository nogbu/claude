## Product learnings from the inventory links (read 2026-09-25)

Labels are the primary source; where a product page differs, both are noted.

**TPS Silica Gold** (label + product page)

- 0-0-1.3. Soluble silicon 3.0%, potash 1.3%, from potassium silicate.  
- Add it to the water **first**, before any other nutrient.  
- Label rates: 5–10 mL/gal once a week, or 2–4 mL/gal every watering. Foliar: 10 mL/gal, 1–2× a week. Product page: 2–4 mL/gal standard; foliar 10–20 mL/gal.  
- Target pH 5.5–6.8. Use through the whole life of the plant.  
- In hydro, change the solution every 4–5 days so the silica doesn't bind with other nutrients.  
- Store 50–80°F, dark. Shake well. Useful life up to 2 years.  
- Page: silicon as silicic acid and organo-silicates with extractives of 5 plants; FAQ says no flushing is needed with it.  
- Anthurium research (2026-10-02): silicon raised leaf count, dry weight and roots in tissue-culture plantlets ([Dias et al. 2017](https://doaj.org/article/b337fe80008f4c7da6aac14bb848516d), 0.5–2 mg/L); reduced bacterial blight severity in deflasked plantlets ([ASHS 2018](https://ashs.confex.com/ashs/2018/webprogramarchives/Paper29431.html)); foliar silicon raised flower counts ([IUT thesis](https://thesis.iut.ac.ir/node/45181), 25–50 mg/L). No semi-hydro study found.

**TPS CalMag Complete** (label + product page)

- 4.0-0-0 (all nitrate N). Ca 4.5%, Mg 1.6%, plus Fe, Mn, Zn, B, Cu, S. Sold as "double strength."  
- Label rates: 5–10 mL/gal once a week, or 1–3 mL/gal every watering. Foliar 10 mL/gal. Product page: 4 mL/gal once a week (twice in heavy growth); 4 mL/gal as a buffer for RO water.  
- Mix with other concentrates only once diluted in water. Jar test small batches when combining with other brands.  
- Target pH 5.5–6.8. Store below 75°F, out of sun; page says 18-month shelf life.  
- Page says 4.5% N, Fe 0.01, S 0.05; the label says 4.0% N, Fe 0.02, S 0.06. Label storage 50–80°F, useful life 2 years; page 18 months.

**TPS One** (label + product page)

- 3.2-6.5-4.1 complete one-part nutrient (2.2% nitrate N, 1.0% ammoniacal N). Ca 1.0%, Mg 0.5%, plus micros and kelp.  
- Contains live microbes: five *Bacillus* species (about 120 million cfu/mL total) plus four mycorrhizae strains.  
- Label: 5–15 mL/gal with every watering, then adjust pH to 5.8–6.2. Suited to inert media (coco, peat, hydro).  
- Mixed solution is reservoir-stable for about **one week**. Check pH often; RO or high-carbonate water can make pH creep.  
- Product page: about 60–75 ppm per mL/gal; pH settles around 5.8–6.0 at 5–6 mL/gal. Most growers use it alone plus pH adjusters. **No extra CalMag needed** in normal use. Optional add-ons: Silica Gold, and Signal in bloom; FAQ (read 2026-10-08): growers add Signal to TPS One in bloom 'at about 2mL per gallon', and a common TPS One feed chart is '2mL Signal in bloom.'  
- Never mix concentrates together; add each to water separately. Use within 12 months. Skin/eye irritant: wear gloves and eye protection.  
- Derived-from includes urea triazone (slow-release urea), though the analysis lists only nitrate and ammoniacal N; TPS says it uses 'slow release nutrition that requires microbes to aid in digestion.' Page says P 6.8% vs the label's 6.5%. Page (re-read 2026-10-02): hydro/DWC feed charts assume fresh feed water every 5–7 days; 'CalMag is hard to overdo'; above 5–6 mL/gal 'the pH will drop and you'll need to use pH Up'; feed by mL per gallon, not PPM.

**TPS Root Boost** (label + product page; product page re-checked 2026-09-26)

- 1.4-3.2-0.7 rooting supplement (monoammonium phosphate + *Ascophyllum* kelp). Contains five *Bacillus* species.  
- Label: 5 mL/gal once a week, or 1–2 mL/gal every watering; seedling/cutting to early flowering. Start when rooting begins.  
- Product page: use once a seedling has rooted and is about 1" tall, or on rooted cuttings. Ornamental plants and trees: 10 mL/gal once monthly. **Indoor orchids: 2 mL/gal every watering.** Cloners: 5 mL/gal.  
- **Root rot (product page):** in **hydro systems**, "a 2-3x dosage of Root Boost for a couple of weeks"; in **soil/coco/peat**, "three times the recommended dose… for two to three weeks." TPS doesn't state which base dose to multiply. *(inference)* Using the orchid rate as the base, 2–3× = 4–6 mL/gal.  
- **H₂O₂ (product page, re-read 2026-10-02):** for root rot in hydro, TPS pairs the 2–3× dose with 'around 1mL per gallon of the 30% H2O2 or 7mL per gallon of the 3% H2O2' in the reservoir, and says Root Boost's bacteria 'produce small amounts of hydrogen peroxide.' The earlier note that frequent H₂O₂ reduces its microbes is no longer on the page. HygroZyme's FAQ caps H₂O₂ at 2 mL/gal; the two sources disagree.  
- Page lists Trichoderma and four Glomus mycorrhizae; the label lists only the five Bacillus (subtilis 20M, pumilus 15M, amyloliquefaciens 15M, licheniformis 35M, laterosporus 35M cfu/mL). Label storage 50–80°F; the page's 45–55°F is stricter.  
- Store cool (ideally 45–55°F, below 70°F). Best within about a year of opening; some mold after opening in warm/humid storage is normal per TPS.

**TPS Signal** (label + product page)

- Potash 3.1%, Mg 0.5%, S 1.0% (from potassium hydroxide and magnesium sulfate). A bloom-stage potassium supplement.  
- Label: 5–10 mL/gal once a week, or 2–5 mL/gal every watering; bloom through harvest. Page: 1–4 mL/gal; half strength when used with TPS One.  
- Mixed solution holds 5–7 days. Target pH 5.5–6.8.  
- TPS gives **no guidance for foliage or non-flowering plants**. *(inference)* Its benefit for anthurium blooms is untested by the source.  
- Page: 17 plant extracts; 'TPS doesn't add any PGRs'; contains a pH buffer; flushing agent at 4–6 mL/gal; use a bubbler for the 5–7-day mix.

**HygroZyme** (product page + FAQ + my label photos)

- Enzyme blend (cellulase, xylanase, hemicellulase, beta-glucanase) that breaks down dead root matter. Preventive for root rot, **not a cure**.  
- Label (my 1 L bottle, original formula, lot 6027-250523, exp 2027-11-23; photographed 2026-10-02): hydro and soil media 2–2.5 mL/L (0.27–0.34 fl oz/gal, 8–10 mL/gal) with every watering or per feeding schedule; never exceed 4 mL/L (15 mL/gal). Propagation: soak trays and media in 1.5 mL/L before use. 'Not a fertilizer or nutrient.' A 5× concentrate exists (1.9 mL/gal); mine is the original.  
- Product pH 4–4.5 (FAQ), so it lowers the pH of unbuffered base water.  
- Label safety: may cause skin, eye and respiratory irritation; avoid breathing mist; harmful if swallowed. Store cool and dry, not above 90°F for extended periods.  
- Works with all nutrients and with beneficial microbes (the enzymes turn dead roots into sugars that feed them).  
- **H₂O₂ is not recommended with it**; if used, max 2 mL/gal (of 3% H₂O₂).  
- Out of sun. 2-year shelf life; don't raise the dose for older product. Survives freezing.  
- The nutrient program PDF had no readable text (likely image-only).

**SLF-100** (product page)

- Enzyme cleanser that removes salt buildup in soilless media and hydro systems. OMRI listed; pH-neutral concentrate; no NPK or sugars.  
- Rates: media/soil 1 tsp/gal weekly; hydro ½ tsp/gal after feeding or water changes; salt flush up to 2× rate daily for 3 days.  
- Compatible with all nutrients, rooting compounds, and **H₂O₂**.  
- Maker page (re-read 2026-10-02): media 1 tsp (5 mL)/gal, applied thoroughly once a week; hydro ½ tsp (2.5 mL)/gal after feeding or change-out; flush up to 2× daily for three days. Retail label copies (Hydrobuilder, Monster Gardens, Hilo) say up to 3×; HTG adds 2.5 mL/gal for seedlings and cuttings. Some copies say it contains "bacteria & enzymes."  
- No independent test of SLF-100, no ingredient list beyond "enzymes," and no source describing a soak were found (searched 2026-10-02). *(inference)* Enzymes act on organic matter; dissolved mineral salts leave only with the water.

**SNS 209** (product page + label + use guide)

- Systemic pest control. Active: rosemary oil 10.23%; inerts water, soap bark, humic acid. FIFRA 25(b) minimum-risk (not EPA-registered).  
- Targets spider mites, broad/russet mites, thrips, fungus gnats, whiteflies, root aphids, and others.  
- Soil drench: 8 mL (½ Tbsp) per gal. Foliar: about 32 mL (2 Tbsp, \~1 oz) per gal, spray to runoff.  
- Schedules differ by document: the label says to apply every 3–4 days for 21 days, then every 14 days. The use guide's drench (every watering, at least 5 in a row, repeat after 2 weeks) is for infestations ("or until infestation reduces"); it gives no drench maintenance schedule, and says to reapply foliar sprays every 14–28 days as needed. A grower forum reports SNS advising monthly drenches for scale and every 2 weeks for mites (unverified, 2026-10-02). Works in 1–2 weeks on potted plants (2–3 days foliar); lasts up to about 3 weeks.  
- Mixing (use guide): can be added to the reservoir or nutrient tank; add SNS 209 first, avoid high-pH products, and jar test any mix; warm (70°F), chlorine-free water absorbs best.  
- Mixed solution keeps up to 2 weeks. Test on a small area first; some varieties are sensitive. Eye irritant; wear gloves and eye protection. Don't freeze.

**Dip 'N Grow** (label + FAQ)

- Rooting hormone: IBA 1.0% + NAA 0.5%, in denatured alcohol (**flammable**).  
- Softwood cuttings: 1 part to 20 parts water. Medium: 1:10. Hardwood: 1:5. When unsure, go weaker; too strong can burn cuttings.  
- Dip the cut end 3–5 seconds, then plant right away. Use the dilution within 10–12 hours; never pour it back into the bottle or dip into the concentrate.  
- Unopened: best within 3 years; darkening is normal. Label covers ornamentals only.  
- FAQ (read 2026-10-01): the maker says it's made for cuttings, not drenches, and that rooting media gain nothing from fertilizer before roots form.

**General Hydroponics RapidStart** (product page + my label screenshots, label ©2017, code LB91230A, F-2262)

- **1-0.5-1:** total N 1.0% (0.3% ammoniacal, 0.7% nitrate); available phosphate (P₂O₅) 0.5%; soluble potash (K₂O) 1.0%; molybdenum (Mo) 0.001%. Derived from ammonium molybdate, ammonium nitrate, monopotassium phosphate, phosphoric acid, and potassium nitrate. Net weight 0.29 lb (0.13 kg).  
- **Purpose (label):** "promotes root growth while enhancing plant vigor and yield." Product page: a rooting supplement for cuttings, seedlings, and young transplants; many growers taper off once a strong root mass forms.  
- **Normal rate:** 1 tsp per 5 gal (¼ mL/L, about 1 mL/gal), for coco coir, soil and soilless, continuous liquid feed.  
- **Aggressive rate:** 2 tsp per 5 gal (½ mL/L, about 1.9 mL/gal), for hydroponics, cloning machines, propagation trays, and root drenches.  
- Rates are for standalone use; when mixing with other products or base nutrients, the label points to genhydro.com feed programs. Add to diluted solution only, never to concentrates; check pH/EC after mixing. Mix thoroughly.  
- **Warnings:** contains molybdenum (crops high in Mo can be toxic to ruminant animals; use only per directions). Keep out of reach of children.  
- **Storage:** cool, dark place, above 40°F; don't freeze. About 2-year unopened shelf life.  
- **Rooting hormone / rooting agent research (2026-09-26):**  
  - **Not a rooting hormone.** No auxin (IBA, NAA) or other growth regulator appears on my label, the GH product page, retailer listings, or the 2010 MSDS. It's sold as a fertilizer (1-0.5-1) with no EPA plant-regulator registration number; EPA's 2020 [draft guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-11/documents/pbs-guidance-updated-draft-guidance-document-2020-11-13_0.pdf) says traditional plant hormones such as auxins require pesticide registration.  
  - **2010 MSDS** ([GH RapidStart MSDS, 12/22/10](https://assets.1000bulbs.com/pdf/gh-gh1703-msds.pdf)): "Hydroponic plant nutrient"; "derived from ammonium nitrate, potassium nitrate, potassium phosphate, iron DPTA, it also contains non-plant food ingredients, 0.05 alfalfa extract, 0.05 barley extract, 0.1 willow bark extract." Units aren't stated *(inference: likely percent)*. The exact formula is a trade secret.  
  - **Formula may have changed.** My ©2017 label's derived-from list differs from the 2010 MSDS (adds ammonium molybdate and phosphoric acid; no iron DTPA), so the willow bark extract may not still be in my bottle. Retailers (Planet Natural, GrowGoods, RASA, GrowersHouse) still describe "plant extracts, amino acids, and nutrients"; GrowersHouse says it's "not a rooting gel" and "the extracts and amino acids do the work."  
  - **Willow bark evidence:** Kawase (1970s) found willow stem extract increased rooting in stem cuttings of many species; the active compound was never identified ([UF/IFAS Plant Propagation](https://propg.ifas.ufl.edu/05-cuttings/01-terminology/11-cuttingterms-rhizocaline.html)). [Wise, Gill & Selby-Pham 2020, *Scientia Horticulturae* 263:109108](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scienta.2019.109108) (open access): on lavender and chrysanthemum cuttings, "1.06 μL/L willow bark extract achieved best results for both species," and "willow bark extract promotes root branching more than adventitious root formation." The study also tested Nutrifield's commercial Root Nectar®; author affiliations weren't checked.  
  - **Dose comparison** *(inference; Claude's math, assuming the 2010 formula and 0.1%)*: at \~2 mL/gal RapidStart delivers \~0.5 µL/L willow extract; at \~1 mL/gal, \~0.25 µL/L. That's about half the study's best dose at the aggressive rate.  
  - **No independent test of RapidStart itself** was found; the one review located (PlantPodLife) was anecdotal.  
  - **Verdict:** not a rooting hormone; its "rooting agent" claim has a plausible basis (willow bark extract, mainly aiding root branching) but is unproven for the product and untested on anthuriums.  
  - Re-checked 2026-10-02: GH's current page gives no analysis or rates; it claims 'fast, dense root initiation' for seedlings, cuttings and young transplants, from propagation through early vegetative growth, then taper off. Hawthorne's listing instead says 'recommended for use during the entire growing cycle.' GH's feed charts couldn't be read.

**Captain Jack's Deadbug Brew Concentrate** (label + product page)

- Spinosad insecticide, EPA Reg. No. 4-471. Targets caterpillars, thrips, leafminers, and other foliage feeders; product page also lists spider mites.  
- Concentrate: 2 fl oz per gallon; spray foliage to runoff; typically 4–7 days between applications, with a seasonal max.  
- ⚠️ **The label says "For Outdoor Residential Use Only,"** so indoor houseplant use isn't covered.  
- Toxic to bees for 3 hours after spraying and to aquatic invertebrates. Store in the original container, locked, away from heat.

**Physan 20** (label + application guide + hydroponics page)

- Quaternary ammonium disinfectant/algaecide/fungicide (two quats at 10% each). EPA Reg. No. 55364-5.  
- Plant use: 1–2 tsp/gal for smaller plants and flowers; spray top and bottom of leaves until wet; weekly to every 2 weeks as prevention, 2–3× a week for active infections. Covers orchids, begonias, African violets, ferns, and others.  
- Hydro: cleaning 1 Tbsp/gal; algae on planted substrate 1.5 tsp/gal; unplanted substrate 1 Tbsp/gal, then wait 7 days before planting. Keep surfaces wet 10 minutes. Reapply for algae every 7 days if needed.  
- **Don't exceed the rate on planted systems** (crop damage). Not an insecticide. Adding it to organic nutrient solutions can cancel its effect.  
- The 16 oz label excerpt lacked the dilution panel, and the gallon hang tag PDF had no readable text.  
- Application guide lists soil-drench root-rot uses for African violets, cacti and succulents; nothing for hydro.  
- Root-rot dips (Physan's orchid and potted-plant pages, read 2026-10-02): orchid rot (e.g. Erwinia): soak the whole plant 10+ minutes at 1 Tbsp/gal, cut away rot with a treated tool, soak the wounds 10 minutes, repot in Physan-dampened medium. Dividing orchids: 2–5 minute soaks at the same rate. Cutting tools: 1 tsp per quart for 10 minutes. Used pots: soak 10 minutes at 1 Tbsp/gal, no rinse. The potted-plant page gives root-rot dips and drenches for cactus, crassula, kalanchoe and sedum (mostly 2 tsp/gal). The hydro page warns that tender roots are at risk from a continual bath of it. Nothing for anthuriums or aroids. A grower reported limp plants after an 8-hour soak (RollItUp forum).  
- Rate check (2026-10-10, read directly from Physan's orchid and potted-plant pages, application guide and EPA label): orchid rot (Erwinia) soak-trim-soak is 1 Tbsp/gal on both pages, as is the 2–5 minute dividing soak; it's also the hard-surface rate. Every orchid spray is lower: adult orchids 2 tsp/gal for disease other than rot (EPA label too); application guide's weekly orchid sprays, soft rot and crown rot included, 1 tsp/gal; botrytis 1 tsp per 3 gal. Hydrobuilder: 1.5 tsp/gal for whole-plant spray, never stronger.

**API Tap Water Conditioner** (label via retailer copy, read 2026-10-01)

- Sodium thiosulfate + tetrasodium EDTA. Chlorine: 1 drop/gal. Chloramine: 3 drops/gal (5 mL per 30 gal).

**TPS pH Down and pH Up** (SDS, read 2026-10-02)

- Compositions withheld as trade secrets. pH Down is pH 0.5 (two proprietary components, 10–30% and 1–5%); pH Up is pH 13 (three proprietary components). Both cause severe skin and eye burns; pH Down is also harmful if inhaled. Incompatibilities: none known. TPS gives no dosing guidance beyond its feedcharts' 5.5–6.2 target.

**Cross-product notes** *(inference, from comparing the sources above)*

- **Microbes vs. sterilizers conflict:** TPS One and Root Boost contain live microbes, which HygroZyme supports and H₂O₂ and Physan 20 kill; using them in the same solution likely cancels the microbes. TPS itself pairs Root Boost with H₂O₂ for hydro root rot (see Root Boost); HygroZyme allows up to 2 mL/gal.  
- **Mixing order:** Silica Gold first, then everything else, each added to water separately.  
- **Ca/Mg overlap:** TPS One already has Ca and Mg, so CalMag Complete is mainly useful with RO water or a diagnosed deficiency.  
- **Mixed-solution shelf life:** Dip 'N Grow 10–12 hours; Silica in hydro 4–5 days; Signal 5–7 days; TPS One about 1 week; SNS 209 up to 2 weeks.  
- **SNS 209 and Silica Gold:** both labels say to add their product first, and SNS 209's guide warns against high-pH products; Silica Gold is potassium silicate, which is strongly alkaline *(inference)*. Jar test before combining.  
- **Root Boost vs. RapidStart** (updated 2026-09-26). They do different jobs and can be used together.  
  - **Root Boost is the biological product:** live *Bacillus* and kelp in monoammonium phosphate (1.4-3.2-0.7). Its role is protecting roots, especially against reinfection; research supports *Bacillus* products mainly as prevention, with modest curative effect (Mattson, e-GRO 2018; McGehee et al. 2019). All of its N is ammonium. Its microbes are harmed by H₂O₂ and Physan 20, so rinse sanitizers off before using it. Store cool (45–55°F ideal). Rates: 1–2 mL/gal every watering (label); 2 mL/gal for orchids (TPS product page); \~25 µS/cm per mL (my reading).  
  - **RapidStart is marketed as the root-growth promoter** (rooting-agent claim plausible but unproven; see its product notes). Its label lists only mineral salts (1-0.5-1, 70% of N as nitrate, plus 0.001% Mo). No live microbes are listed, so *(inference)* sanitizers don't compromise it. Rates: about 1 mL/gal "normal" (1 tsp per 5 gal) and about 1.9 mL/gal "aggressive" for hydroponics and root drenches (2 tsp per 5 gal). Keeps at room temperature above 40°F; don't freeze. \~32 µS/cm per mL/gal (measured 2026-10-02).  
  - **Nutrient contribution** *(inference; Claude's label math)*: both are small. RapidStart at \~1.9 mL/gal adds about 5 ppm N, 1 ppm P, and 4 ppm K. Root Boost at 2 mL/gal adds about 8 ppm N (all ammonium) and 8 ppm P.  
  - **Best use:** both belong where roots are forming (fresh repots, props, rehab, root-rot recovery). For established plants, neither is needed routinely.

## Kelpak (added 2026-10-02)

(researched 2026-10-02; bought 2026-10-02; in putes since v7)

* **What it is:** a liquid concentrate of fresh *Ecklonia maxima* kelp, made by Kelp Products (Pty) Ltd, Simon's Town, South Africa, using a cold "cell burst" extraction (no heat or chemicals). 35–38% fresh kelp extract. Sold as KelpMax in the past; First Rays' 1 L bottle and Kelpak USA's "Liquid Seaweed Concentrate 0-0-1" are the same product (their technical sheets).  
* **Analysis:** 0-0-1 (about 1% K₂O plus naturally occurring micronutrients). The maker says it adds no meaningful nutrients at use rates; it's a biostimulant, not a fertilizer. pH 4.0–4.6; specific gravity 1.01–1.03.  
* **Active compounds:** auxins (IAA and conjugates) dominate over cytokinins, about 360:1 by bioassay (maker) or about 34 vs 5 pmol/mL by chemical analysis (Stirk et al. 2004). Also polyamines, gibberellins, brassinosteroids, abscisic acid, phlorotannins (eckol, phloroglucinol), alginates and amino acids. A high auxin-to-cytokinin ratio favors roots over shoots.  
* **Rooting evidence (independent of favored sources):** root promoters found in the concentrate, which also promoted rooting in six garden species (Crouch & van Staden 1991); a drench after planting out tissue-culture plantlets significantly increased root growth and establishment (Lindsey et al.); best mung bean rooting among commercial seaweed extracts tested, and better than two other *E. maxima* products. A 2024 review of ornamental cuttings found it improved rooting in several trials, though IBA gave the highest rooting percentage in one. Most of this research comes from one university group (KwaZulu-Natal). Dose matters: in one cuttings study, both 1:1 and 1:100 lowered rooting. No anthurium, aroid or semi-hydro study found.  
* **With beneficial microbes:** combined with *Bacillus licheniformis* (one of Root Boost's species), Kelpak cancelled the growth setbacks the bacteria caused alone (amaranth, 2020); it amplified microbial products' benefits in onion seedlings (2024) and improved salt tolerance with a microbial product in watermelon (2026). In hydroponic cucumber, Kelpak plus root microbes improved leaf area and leaf P under P shortage.  
* **Disease:** no study found on Kelpak against Pythium or other root rots. It reduced nematode root damage in tomato (cited in a 2025 review). Other brown-kelp extracts triggered plant defenses against Phytophthora, but those weren't Kelpak.  
* **Rates (First Rays, favored):** 1 Tbsp/gal (1:250, about 4 mL/L); **7.5 mL per ½ gal**. Established plants: no more than once a month (every 2 weeks if pushing growth). Repotting: water in with it, then use it for the next two waterings. Rootless plants: soak the whole plant 1–2 hours in warm solution, pot without rinsing, then two more waterings with it. A single double-strength dose is safe. Diluting to 1:500 or weaker makes it ineffective. Mix it with fertilizer rather than applying alone.  
* **Rates (maker):** in hydroponic systems, no weaker than 1:1000 and no more often than every 10 days; add it last and keep solution pH below 7. Wilted, stressed plants: pour 0.5–1% at the base. Overdosing is unlikely even at 10%.  
* **Sources disagree** (follow First Rays, config rule 2026-10-06): First Rays' repot schedule (day 0 and the next waterings) is closer together than the maker's 10-day minimum, and its minimum strength (1:250 to 1:500) is stricter than the maker's (1:1000).  
* **Storage:** cool and dark; avoid direct sun and temperatures below 32°F or above 95–104°F (SDS and technical sheet differ). Maker: stable 2 years sealed; once opened, use within 3 weeks because microbes can grow in it, though its activity stays intact. First Rays: a conservative 3 years, refrigeration acceptable, current batch expires 2/10/2029. Auxin levels drop in storage at high temperature (Stirk et al. 2004). Diluted, its organic compounds break down within a few days, so use mixed solution right away.  
* **Safety:** non-hazardous, non-irritant, non-toxic to bees, fish, birds and mammals (SDS). **Incompatible with strong bases and oxidizers** (SDS), so keep it away from pH Up concentrate and H₂O₂.  
* **Semi-hydro:** LECA Addict uses it (as KelpMax) monthly to keep roots growing.  
* **Buying:** First Rays, 1 L, $28 (2026-10-02); volume discounts apply automatically; AOS vouchers give a code with free US shipping. Kelpak USA sells quart, 2.5 gal and 275 gal sizes.

Because the solution sits in the reservoir for a week, most of Kelpak's effect likely comes in the first few days. Its 3-week opened life means a 1 L bottle will outlast its best window unless refrigerated.
