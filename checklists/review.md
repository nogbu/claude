# Review checklist

Kinds of mistakes the reviewer checks every draft for. Each one is a kind of mistake, not a single case. The example in brackets is where it came from.

When Nwani catches a mistake the reviewer missed, add its kind here. Don't add the single example.

1. **Comparing things that aren't comparable.** Both sides must be the same kind of thing, equally complete, and from the same point in time. If they aren't, the comparison can't be made yet, or only its fair part can. [A full fantasy team was ranked against half-drafted ones, 2026-10-19.]
2. **Saying something exists or doesn't without looking.** A rule, file, setting, fact or option. Search first. [Said there was no rule when there was one.]
3. **Stating a fact a source could confirm, without checking the source.** [Gave a fertilizer dosage that was wrong while the right one sat in a learnings file.]
4. **Answering the literal question instead of the goal behind it.** [Ranked teams because that was asked, when the point was knowing where the team really stood.]
5. **Presenting a partial result as complete.** If the answer rests on partial data, a sample or a cut-off read, say so up front. [Read only the first 250 characters of search results.]
6. **More confidence than the evidence supports.** Guesses and inferences are labeled as such.
7. **Missing the basic, well-known options.** Before recommending, check that the standard approaches were considered. [Dozens of speed ideas, none of them running tasks in parallel.]
8. **Treating silence or a change of subject as a yes.** Only Nwani's answer counts as approval. [Saved a change after the subject changed.]
9. **Narrowing a general problem to the example given.** When Nwani describes a problem with an example, the fix must cover the whole kind of problem. [Built a fix for data comparisons when the problem was every kind of answer, 2026-10-20.]
10. **Overstating.** A word like nothing or never must be literally true. If the real claim is narrower, say the narrower thing. [Said claude.ai runs nothing from a repo in project threads, when Claude runs repo scripts there itself, 2026-10-22.]
11. **Not explaining.** The reply itself must explain in plain words what happened and why, and what each item is. Keeping a reply short is never a reason to leave that out. A file or link can add detail, but Nwani shouldn't need it, or need to remember earlier messages, to follow the reply. [A fresh look at every open issue gave steps and a file link but no explanation, 2026-10-21.]
12. **Unchecked claims on a card.** A card's options and reason are claims too, and the reviewer never sees them. Check each one against its source before posting, including everywhere the thing it describes is used. [Called a dose short-term on a card when the same batch is also a monthly refill for other plants, 2026-10-25.]
13. **Losing an open request.** A draft that quotes, copies or changes something an earlier request covers says whether that request is done, and if not, what's left. [Copied putes and flutes and quoted inventory's inferences while a request to verify every inference sat unfinished in another thread, 2026-10-25.]
14. **Leaving Nwani a step Claude could take.** When the next step is something Claude can do, like merging a pull request, offer to do it on a card instead of telling Nwani to do it. A merge that a save already approved needs no card. Claude just merges it. [Said a recipe would be reposted "once you merge it" without offering to merge, 2026-10-25.]
15. **More than one card at a time.** Only one card is open at once. Before posting a card, withdraw any card that's still open. Its question stays in the queue. [Posted three cards in a row while two were already open, 2026-10-25.]
16. **Acting on a guess about what Nwani meant.** If a request, upload or instruction could mean more than one thing, or leaves out what to do, the draft asks on a card instead of acting.
17. **Asking what the request already settles.** When Nwani's words already say what to do, do it. A card covers only the part the words leave open, and its options stay inside what was asked. Things the request doesn't cover aren't options. If it's unclear whether something falls under the request, that part is left open (item 16) and goes on a card. Check every card against this before posting it, as in item 12. [Asked whether to delete the negative lists Nwani had asked to delete, and offered to delete reports and research that weren't lists, 2026-11-05.]
18. **Bringing up what the request doesn't cover.** Don't mention things outside the request just to say they weren't touched or don't apply, like another client's files in a client's thread. [Said Delta Dental's negative lists weren't touched after deleting 7FAM's in the 7FAM thread, 2026-11-05.]
19. **Calling a task done before Nwani says so.** Only Nwani closes a task, by saying it's done or picking a card option that says so. Until then, a draft doesn't call the task finished, move on to queued questions, re-ask them, post a "Still open:" list or resolve the thread (a thread started only to run a test still resolves itself once it reports, and a thread full of files still resolves itself when it hands over). When the work looks finished, it asks on a Done card instead. [Declared a task done and moved on to queued questions while Nwani hadn't said it was done, in the earlier New claude repo design thread.]
20. **A list strung through a sentence.** Three or more things joined by commas and "and" or "or" go on bullets, one per line, in replies and on cards. The reply check catches "a, b, and c" and number lists like "2, 5 and 6". Look for the ones it can't spot, like "a, b and c" with no comma before "and", lists of long clauses or of steps, and any list on a card. [Listed three open questions inside one sentence in a fixed draft, and a same-reviewer recheck passed it, 2026-11-07.]
21. **Numbers without their metric.** Every number in a reply names its metric (conversions, clicks, cost, impressions) next to it, ideally as a table column, not only in a heading or an earlier line. [Listed term changes as "+190", "−74" and "22 to 11" without saying they were conversions, 2026-11-10.]
22. **Adding extras the request didn't ask for.** Before building, list what the request asks for, in its own words. Any grouping, breakdown, column, tab, chart or analysis beyond that list is an extra: it is not built or shown. It goes only in the numbered extras offer at the end, followed by "Typed answer: the numbers to include" (CLAUDE.md, Optimize). The reviewer checks every part of the draft and its attachments against that list. [Asked for which terms gained or lost conversions and volume, Claude invented term groups, a campaign breakdown and an extra sheet tab, 2026-11-10.]
23. **More than one decision on a Save card.** A Save card holds one decision. The edits that decision needs go on the same card, and the reply before it shows them. For example, a rule change brings its manual line with it. Separate decisions, even related ones, get one Save card each, in the order they depend on each other. [Put five open-questions rule changes on one Save card, 2026-11-11.]
