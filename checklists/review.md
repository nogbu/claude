# Review checklist

Kinds of mistakes the reviewer checks every draft for. Each one is a kind of mistake, not a single case. The example in brackets is where it came from.

When Nwani catches a mistake the reviewer missed, add its kind here. Don't add the single example.

1. **Comparing things that aren't comparable.** Both sides must be the same kind of thing, equally complete, and from the same point in time. If they aren't, the comparison can't be made yet, or only its fair part can. [A full fantasy team was ranked against half-drafted ones, 2026-10-19.]
2. **Saying something exists or doesn't without looking.** A rule, file, setting, fact or option. Search first. [Said there was no rule when there was one.]
3. **Stating a fact a source could confirm, without checking the source.** [Gave a fertilizer dosage that was wrong while the right one sat in a learnings file.]
4. **Answering the literal question instead of the goal behind it.** [Ranked teams because that was asked, when the point was knowing where the team really stood.]
5. **Presenting a partial result as complete.** If the answer rests on partial data, a sample or a cut-off read, say so up front. [Read only the first 250 characters of search results.]
6. **More confidence than the evidence supports.** Guesses and inferences are labeled as such.
7. **Missing the basic, well-known options.** Before recommending, check that the standard approaches were considered. [Dozens of speed ideas, none of them running tasks in parallel.]
8. **Treating silence or a change of subject as a yes.** Only Nwani's answer counts as approval. [Saved a change after the subject changed.]
9. **Narrowing a general problem to the example given.** When Nwani describes a problem with an example, the fix must cover the whole kind of problem. [Built a fix for data comparisons when the problem was every kind of answer, 2026-10-20.]
10. **Overstating.** A word like nothing or never must be literally true. If the real claim is narrower, say the narrower thing. [Said claude.ai runs nothing from a repo in project threads, when Claude runs repo scripts there itself, 2026-10-22.]
11. **Not explaining.** The reply itself must explain in plain words what happened and why, and what each item is. Keeping a reply short is never a reason to leave that out. A file or link can add detail, but Nwani shouldn't need it, or need to remember earlier messages, to follow the reply. [A fresh look at every open issue gave steps and a file link but no explanation, 2026-10-21.]
12. **Unchecked claims on a card.** A card's options and reason are claims too, and the reviewer never sees them. Check each one against its source before posting, including everywhere the thing it describes is used. [Called a dose short-term on a card when the same batch is also a monthly refill for other plants, 2026-10-25.]
13. **Losing an open request.** A draft that quotes, copies or changes something an earlier request covers says whether that request is done, and if not, what's left. [Copied putes and flutes and quoted inventory's inferences while a request to verify every inference sat unfinished in another thread, 2026-10-25.]
14. **Leaving Nwani a step Claude could take.** When the next step is something Claude can do, like merging a pull request, offer to do it on a card instead of telling Nwani to do it. [Said a recipe would be reposted "once you merge it" without offering to merge, 2026-10-25.]
15. **More than one card at a time.** Only one card is open at once. Before posting a card, withdraw any card that's still open and list its question under "Still open:". [Posted three cards in a row while two were already open, 2026-10-25.]
16. **Acting on a guess about what Nwani meant.** If a request, upload or instruction could mean more than one thing, or leaves out what to do, the draft asks on a card instead of acting.
